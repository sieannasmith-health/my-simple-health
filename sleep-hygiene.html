Sleep Hygiene: Simple Habits for Better Sleep

Better sleep is not about creating a perfect nighttime routine. Small changes to your schedule, environment, and daily habits can help create conditions that support healthy sleep.

My Simple Health • 6 min read

Sleep hygiene refers to the everyday habits and environmental conditions that support healthy sleep. It is not a cure-all for every sleep problem, but it can create a stronger foundation for getting enough, good-quality rest.

Start with enough time for sleep

Healthy sleep begins with giving yourself enough opportunity to sleep. Adults ages 18 to 60 generally need at least seven hours of sleep each night, although individual needs can vary.

Instead of focusing only on what time you fall asleep, work backward from when you need to wake up and protect enough time for rest.

SIMPLE TAKEAWAY

A better bedtime routine cannot compensate for consistently giving yourself too little time to sleep.

Keep your sleep and wake times consistent

Your body operates on an internal sleep-wake rhythm. Going to bed and waking up around the same time each day can help reinforce that rhythm.

Your schedule does not need to be exact to the minute. Think consistency rather than perfection. Large shifts between weekdays and weekends can make it harder for your body to maintain a predictable rhythm.

Use light to support your body clock

Light is one of the signals your body uses to distinguish daytime from nighttime. Spending time outdoors and getting natural light earlier in the day can support your sleep-wake cycle.

In the evening, begin reducing bright light and stimulation as bedtime approaches. That does not mean your home needs to become completely dark at 7 p.m. It simply means creating a gradual transition from an active day toward rest.

Create a bedroom that supports sleep

Your sleep environment matters. A bedroom that is relatively cool, quiet, dark, and comfortable can make it easier to settle into sleep.

Consider what regularly disrupts your sleep. Streetlights, notifications, television, noise, uncomfortable bedding, or a room that feels too warm may be small problems individually but meaningful when they happen every night.

Try a quick bedroom check

  • Is the room dark enough?
  • Is the temperature comfortable?
  • Are unnecessary notifications silenced?
  • Can outside noise be reduced?
  • Does the space feel calm and comfortable?

Watch caffeine later in the day

Caffeine is a stimulant, and its effects can continue for hours. For some people, afternoon or evening caffeine can make falling asleep more difficult.

You do not necessarily have to eliminate coffee or tea. Pay attention to timing and to your own response. If falling asleep is difficult, moving your last caffeinated drink earlier may be a useful experiment.

Let movement support your sleep

Regular daytime physical activity is another habit that can support healthy sleep. That movement does not have to mean an intense gym workout.

Walking, recreational activity, strength training, household movement, and other forms of physical activity can all contribute to an active day.

Build a wind-down routine you can actually repeat

A bedtime routine works best when it is realistic enough to do regularly. It does not need ten steps, special products, or an elaborate wellness ritual.

Your routine might simply be lowering the lights, putting your phone away, washing your face, preparing for the next morning, and reading for a few minutes.

The specific activities matter less than creating a predictable transition between being awake and preparing to sleep.

MAKE IT SIMPLE

Choose two or three calming things you already enjoy and use them consistently instead of trying to build the perfect bedtime routine.

What if sleep is still difficult?

Healthy sleep habits can support sleep, but persistent sleep problems may need more than lifestyle changes.

If you regularly have trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, experience significant daytime sleepiness, or have another ongoing concern about your sleep, consider discussing it with a healthcare professional.

YOUR SIMPLE ACTION PLAN

Choose one change tonight.

You do not need to overhaul your entire routine. Start with one habit that feels realistic.

01

Choose a consistent wake time.

02

Dim lights before bed.

03

Move caffeine earlier.

04

Make your bedroom quieter, cooler, or darker.
